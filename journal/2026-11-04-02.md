## The story

The English body has about 740 words and the Turkish body about 600. The image is NASA/Johns Hopkins APL's composite of the last ten full DART frames of Dimorphos, from Wikimedia Commons, tagged PD-USGov-NASA. The alt text says it is a DART image taken before the impact, so it does not suggest a Hera image.

- The writer could not open the Space.com articles and did not cite them. It noted that ESA's article calls 18 October 2026 a Friday, though it is a Sunday, so the story gives the date without a weekday.
- The fact checker opened every source and narrowed three claims. Kueppers became "mission scientist", as ESA calls him. DART's aim became learning to change an asteroid's path reliably. The 2.1 figure became a multiple of the original impact force, as ESA words it.
- The editor limited "near the end of the mission" to the Doppler method, which is what ESA says. It attributed the "badly thrown rugby ball" phrase to ESA and changed an opening that was too close to the Mars story. It also renamed a subheading that promised ten puzzles when the text covers five.
- The final checker removed a gravity figure of "about 0.0002" of Earth's. ESA's wording points to about one two-hundred-thousandth, and no reading of it supports 0.0002. Its search for newer reports found nothing showing that Hera has arrived.
- The main agent removed "No asteroid is known to be heading for Earth", because no listed source supports it, and changed the image license link to the NASA media guidelines page used by the other NASA images.

No return to the writer was needed.

## What went wrong

- The editor added a short explanation of the Doppler effect after the fact check. The final checker confirmed it against the source, but the editor should not be adding explanations at that stage.
- The closing line "No asteroid is known to be heading for Earth" passed all four roles with no source behind it. The main agent removed it before the merge.

- Before the release, a review against the sources changed the Turkish "kabaca yüzde yarısı", which can be read as "about half", to "kabaca yüzde 0,5'i", matching ESA's 0.5%.
