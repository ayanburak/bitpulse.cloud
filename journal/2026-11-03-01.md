## Why

The owner's request in HANDOFF.md came first. The latest published piece was an explainer, so this run owed a news story, and HANDOFF.md asked for a subject other than AI chatbots. The writer chose the first century tomb found inside the ancient theater of Apollonia ad Rhyndacum at Gölyazı, Bursa. It is a recent event with a clear open question, it falls in a category the last two news stories did not use, and it is a Turkish subject. That makes it the first small test of whether local Turkish stories earn Turkish readers.

## Statistics

/stats/index.json lists 2026-10-31, 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-02. 2026-11-01 has 57 views and 2026-11-02 has 5. As HANDOFF.md records, these include the owner's own devices and links shared privately, so they do not show reach. On 2026-11-01 Turkish pages had most of the counted views, with 18 on /tr/ against 5 on /en/, and www.google.com sent 3 referrals. Bot views were 255 and 106. Both days show about 41 not found responses, and the statistics do not say which addresses they were. No decision in this run rests on these numbers.

## The story

The writer's English version has about 685 words and the Turkish version 505. The Turkish length test minimum is 500, so the editor could not cut anything from the Turkish text. All the detail comes from the excavation director, Prof. Derya Şahin of Bursa Uludağ University, through Anadolu Agency copy and local and specialist outlets. No official statement or paper exists yet. Her idea that the grave had a sacred or heroic meaning is presented as her suggestion. The image is a CC BY-SA 4.0 Wikimedia Commons photo of Gölyazı at sunset, because the Commons photos of the theater were too small. The alt text says it shows the village and not the dig.

The fact check passed with one wording fix. The text had said the dig would let the team "map" the base of the seats, and the sources say it will identify the seating foundations. The editor changed only the headline and the summary. It shortened the headline to end in a bare "Why?". The final checker restored the earlier headline, because a bare question promises an answer the story can not give. It searched for reports from 1 to 3 November and found none that explains the burial, so the story's statement on that point stands. No return to the writer was needed.

After the final check, the main agent changed one sentence in both languages. It had said the team plans to search historical sources, but that point comes from the reporter of one local source and not from the team. It now says that the historical sources have yet to be searched.

## Tried or proposed for the mission

- The statistics proposal recommends two steps that need no notice and no consent first. One is a better bot filter in the server's log job. The other is Google Search Console, which would show which language earns search impressions. After that, if depth is wanted, it recommends a first-party script that loads only after a reader presses Accept, with one consent tool shared with AdSense so readers do not see two banners. It advises against Google Analytics 4, because its advanced mode loads code before a choice and agents could only read it with tokens. Whether Google's AdSense consent tool can control a script that is not from Google, and whether it can show a notice in Turkey, could not be confirmed. The proposal has no branch yet, because the banner's design depends on that answer.
- The proposal also notes that the privacy page says the aggregated statistics are published in the project's public repository. They are published at bitpulse.cloud/stats, and this repository holds none of them.
- The not found count, about 41 a day, may hide broken links or old addresses that cost readers. A list of the most requested missing paths in the statistics would show which. This is in Owner actions.
- A local Turkish news story was published as a test of the Turkish reader idea. Its result can be read only after a release.
- The research on explainer questions and the in-body links from HANDOFF.md were not done. The usage went to the proposal and the story, and both tasks were carried over.

## What went wrong

- The editor pushed an intermediate commit to the draft branch while the Turkish length test was failing. Later commits fixed it, and main never held a failing commit.
- The editor's headline overpromised. The final check caught it.
- The final checker spotted a sentence that credited the team with a plan that came from a reporter, and left it in place. The main agent corrected it after the final check. That correction was not checked again by a separate agent.
- The KVKK cookie guide, the ePrivacy text and the EDPB consent guidelines could not be opened, so the Turkish consent rules in the proposal rest on law firm summaries and are marked as unconfirmed.
- The review after the run corrected the story before release. It had said the lower part of the building was in much better shape and largely complete, while the sources say that its excavation is largely complete. The fact checker and the final checker both passed that sentence. The same review softened "very few" finds to "few", fixed one Turkish sentence and corrected the title of the Politikam source.
- The research tool returned summaries of several pages instead of their full text. No claim in the story rests only on such a page.
