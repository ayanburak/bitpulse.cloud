## The story

The English body has about 1,650 words and the Turkish body about 1,380. The image is a frame of an AMD Epyc server with its memory modules installed, by 极客湾Geekerwan, from Wikimedia Commons under CC BY 3.0. It carries the creator's small channel logo in one corner.

- The writer built the piece on Micron's fourth quarter results filed with the SEC, Micron's announcement of its exit from Crucial, SK hynix's third quarter release and TrendForce reports. Micron's own release site timed out, and its investor site copy was cited instead.
- The fact checker found that several Samsung and SK hynix figures came from Korean outlets relayed by TrendForce, not from the companies, and attributed them that way. It replaced "American memory maker", which no opened page supported, with Micron's Boise dateline, and corrected 768 GiB, "low to mid single-digit" and the description of Crucial.
- The editor rewrote a two sentence headline, cut two outlet attributions together with the figures that rested on them, explained spot prices and removed an ending that echoed the quantum explainer.
- The final checker changed the headline again, because the editor's version suggested data centers buy the same chips as laptops. It narrowed the summary and the closing section to what SK hynix, Samsung and TrendForce each said, restored two outlet names that the editor had shortened to "Korean press" and fixed four Turkish details. Its search found Samsung's results of 29 October, a TrendForce report of 30 October and a spot price update of 4 November. All of them fit the text. It also found an older Bloomberg Intelligence view that the shortage may have peaked, which is why the closing no longer speaks for "analysts" in general.
- The main agent corrected one sentence before the merge. It said Macronix moved capacity toward AI-related memory, but the Macronix story says the capacity goes to NAND, which it sells mostly into communications and consumer electronics. The sentence now says only that factory lines are limited and go to the fastest growing memory.

No return to the writer was needed.

## Tried or proposed for the mission

- The question in the headline is a common search during a price spike. Search Console can show after the release whether the page gets impressions for it.
- The two way link between the explainer and the Macronix story gives a reader who arrives at either one a second page to read.
- The not found responses tripled in a day. A short list of the most requested missing paths, in aggregated form, would show whether readers hit broken links or whether bots probe for files. The statistics job runs on the server, so this is listed as an owner action.
- The explainer depends on market data that will change. When TrendForce or the memory makers publish new figures, a news story can report them and link back, and the explainer can carry a dated correction note if a figure turns out to be wrong.

## What went wrong

- Again, a sentence tied Macronix's capacity shift to AI memory, which the Macronix story does not say. It passed the writer, the fact checker, the editor and the final checker, because none of them was asked to compare the link sentence with the linked story. The main agent caught it. A sentence that summarises an earlier Bitpulse story needs the same check against that story as any other claim.
- The editor shortened two attributions to "Korean press", so the reader could no longer see who made the claims. The final checker restored them.
- The Samsung and SK hynix figures beyond the official releases come from Korean outlets as relayed by TrendForce, not from transcripts. The text says so.
