## The story

The first draft said in its headline, summary and opening that the crew had walked out after 378 days. The fact checker sent it back, because the only source for the exit was a weak aggregator, and the NASA release and other coverage were written before the event. A new writer looked for a better source and found none, so the story was rebuilt around what NASA wants to learn, with the exit as a scheduled event. The weak source was removed, along with two claims that rested on it alone: the two weeks of debriefs and the statement that no results had been published.

The second fact check changed tenses that implied the stay was over, added NASA's Deep Space Network page as a source and corrected the context of two quotations. The editor rewrote the headlines and summaries. The final checker made eight corrections. The story now says that the date has passed and that NASA's website had not reported the outcome as of November 1. It also removed a claim about the habitat's next project and softened wording the sources did not support. One of the two allowed returns was used.

The result is a weaker news story than the first draft promised. It was published because it is accurate and it tells the reader plainly what is not known. The English version has about 760 words and the Turkish version about 620, counted on the source files.

## Corrected before release

The story was rewritten after this run, before any reader could see it. The final check of the run said the outcome had not been reported, but Houston Public Media had reported the crew's exit from the site on October 31. Its report was added as a source, and the headline, summary, opening, tenses and ending now say that the crew left the habitat. The earlier search for a source missed that report, which is why the run built the story around a scheduled event.

## Tried or proposed for the mission

Research covered what brings readers to a new site with no audience. Its main findings are below. Many rest on weak evidence, and this is stated where it applies.

- The Turkish edition may be the best opening for a site with no authority, because Turkish search interest in AI is high. This is an inference. No data on competition or search volume was found. Story choice and Turkish headlines now take it into account.
- Stories decay in days. Explainers that answer a lasting question keep earning search visits, and none has been published yet. No hard data on small sites was found.
- Google News no longer uses feeds or web addresses submitted through Publisher Center, according to one trade source. Google's own help page could not be opened. Submitting there is therefore not listed as a task.
- Separate pages for the editorial policy and corrections would help readers and reviewers judge the site. Google advises explaining how automation is used. They need locked files and a visible footer change, so this is the next proposal.
- Flipboard, Bing Webmaster Tools with IndexNow and one human written post about the open newsroom are in Owner actions.
- Reading the live pages for the research led to the broken x-default link on Turkish stories. A check of the live site confirmed the 404, and the fix is on proposal/sitemap-dates.

Three ideas were rejected. A named responsible person conflicts with the editorial policy, which allows no personal names. Mass produced topic pages match Google's description of scaled content abuse. Paid links, engagement groups and mass posting are against the mission. Apple News, MSN, Reddit and Turkish aggregators could not be confirmed and were left out.

## What went wrong

- The writer chose a story whose central event had happened hours earlier and could not yet be confirmed. This cost one full return and a second fact check, about 66,000 tokens. Stories built on an event that happens today need a good source for the event before writing starts.
- The first fact check was told to treat the headline as a claim and caught the problem, but left three corrections uncommitted when it returned the story, as the role requires. The next writer kept them.
- The story that reached the final checker did not yet say that the exit date had gone by. The final checker added this, so the most important sentence for honest reading arrived at the last step.
- The research agent gave wrong first impressions of the site, because its fetch tool reports rendered text and missed the page metadata. It corrected this by reading the raw HTML.
- The proposal agent found the x-default bug and left it alone. The main agent fixed it on the branch and did this code work itself, outside the model table.
- A leftover worktree from an earlier run remains at /tmp/bitpulse-run on proposal/search-and-sharing-metadata. It is outside the repository and affects nothing.
