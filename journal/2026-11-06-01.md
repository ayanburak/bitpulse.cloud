## Why

The manual changed before this run: four news stories are now published for every explainer, and a news story is chosen only when real, unbroken footage of its event exists. The latest piece was a news story, so this run published a news story. The writer chose the spacewalk of 5 November 2026 in which Anil Menon and Joshua Kutryk installed the eighth and final roll-out solar array on the International Space Station. NASA streamed the whole spacewalk, and the array unrolling is one unbroken moment of the event. The three pieces before it were games, economy and games, so a science story added variety.

The owner's request comes first in a run, and it asked for research on which categories draw interest worldwide, with the footage rule in mind, proposed as a design proposal.

The main sources are two NASA station blog posts and two Spaceflight Now reports of 5 November, plus the NASA+ listing of the broadcast.

## Tried or proposed for the mission

- The category proposal keeps the five categories and their keys, so the menu does not change. It gives each category a focus drawn from the research: AI, robots, devices and vehicles in Technology; space, nature, health research and the Earth in Science; trailers, consoles and studios in Games; film, television, music and internet culture in Culture. It narrows Economy to companies, products, prices and trade connected to the other four. News without footage, such as figures, filings and regulations, goes to explainers. The strongest evidence: social and video networks (54%) passed news sites (51%) as a way to reach news in the Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026. Science and technology was the topic US news avoiders avoided least in its 2023 report. Only 3% of news influencers' posts were about technology in Pew's 2024 study. Google's 2025 Year in Search was led by AI, phones, games and films. No opened source ranks the five categories by worldwide interest directly, and the proposal says so.
- A space story with a NASA stream is a case where the footage rule and the image rights line up. Public agency footage is free of the rights questions that game and film trailers raise.
