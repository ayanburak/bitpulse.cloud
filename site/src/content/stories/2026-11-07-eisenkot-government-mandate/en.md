Israeli President Isaac Herzog has chosen former military chief Gadi Eisenkot to try to form the next government, his office said in a statement on Saturday. Eisenkot, who leads the Yashar party and heads an alliance against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, will be invited to the president's residence on Sunday for a formal ceremony. The spokesperson said he "will be tasked with forming the government tomorrow".

## A deadlocked result

The October 27 election left neither side with a majority. The Knesset has 120 seats and 61 are needed to govern. Netanyahu's religious-nationalist coalition won 53 and his party, Likud, took 27, the most of any single party. Yashar won 21, and Eisenkot's wider coalition of four Jewish-led parties won 48. Two parties representing Israel's Arab minority won a record 19.

Most parties recommended Eisenkot, according to ABC Australia. Both Arab lists said during the week that they would back him, even though, as Reuters reports, he has declined to join forces with Arab parties. Netanyahu ruled out working with them. Herzog's role is largely ceremonial and consists of nominating a candidate to form a coalition.

## What happens next

Eisenkot has 28 days to build a government, with a possible 14-day extension. Weeks of negotiations are expected. Reuters lists three possible routes: a minority government with outside Arab support, a coalition with ultra-Orthodox parties that he and his allies have shunned in the past, or a unity government with Netanyahu. Analysts say all three are unlikely.

If he fails, another candidate can try, and if that candidate fails, parliament can nominate someone else. The whole process has a deadline of around 100 days, and if nobody succeeds, another election is held.

Eisenkot is 66, the son of Moroccan immigrants, and would be the first prime minister from Israel's Mizrahi community, Jews who trace their roots to the Middle East and North Africa. He entered politics in 2022 and briefly served in Netanyahu's wartime cabinet after the Hamas attack of October 7, 2023. He later returned to the opposition, publicly criticised Netanyahu's leadership and has sworn not to serve in a government with him. A son and two nephews of Eisenkot were killed fighting in Gaza.
