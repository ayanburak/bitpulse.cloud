The United States is offering up to $20 million, and possibly relocation, for information about Iran's nuclear program. US Central Command announced it on X on Friday, November 6.

It said the State Department's Rewards for Justice program is seeking information on Iran's nuclear "facilities, equipment, and associated personnel, such as those in the scientific community." The program was set up in 1984 to pay for information that protects American lives and interests. The State Department gave no details on what information would earn the full amount.

## What the offer says

The Jerusalem Post reports that Rewards for Justice has paid more than $250 million to over 125 people since 1984. It keeps sources confidential and can offer relocation to a source and their family case by case. Tips can be sent through Signal, Telegram and the Tor Browser. The Post also noted it is unclear why the offer came about eight months into the war.

In its statement, the State Department said Iran's lack of compliance with the International Atomic Energy Agency, the UN nuclear watchdog, has led the agency to lose "continuity of knowledge" about Iran's production and stock of key parts such as centrifuges. Centrifuges are the machines that enrich uranium. The statement also cited a 2025 agency report that Iran was enriching uranium to 60 percent purity, which it called a short technical step from the 90 percent needed for a weapon.

## Trump and the talks

President Donald Trump has repeatedly said US forces have effectively obliterated Iran's nuclear program. On Friday night he said that without the strikes on Iran "you wouldn't have Israel today." He also said Iran, whose economy and military he says were badly damaged over eight months of war, wants to end the conflict. "We're dealing with them now. They want to make a deal very badly," he said. Earlier this week he said he still had to "take care" of one or two targets, including a site in central Iran called Pickaxe Mountain. Tehran denies it is a nuclear facility.

Mediators are working to prevent any return to fighting. Iran is reportedly reviewing proposals from Qatar, the chief mediator, on the nuclear dispute. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said on Tuesday that his government is still trying to resolve the crisis through dialogue.

The situation remains tense. Iranian state media reported on Saturday that Iran's air defense destroyed a US MQ-9 drone over Qeshm Island, according to an army statement. The US has not been quoted on that claim in the sources available.

Also on Friday, the US and Canada sanctioned eight digital asset service providers, which the State Department said help Iran evade sanctions.
