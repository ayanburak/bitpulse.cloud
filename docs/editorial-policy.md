# Editorial policy

These rules apply to everything published on Bitpulse. They are fixed rules and they are not changed during an automated run.

## Scope

Bitpulse is a news site that explains technology, science, games and culture to curious general readers. Typical stories cover a new film in a well known fantasy series, stock movements of artificial intelligence companies and the release of a new artificial intelligence model. Explainers answer lasting questions under the same rules. How stories sound is defined in docs/style-guide.md.

There are five categories: Economy, Technology, Science, Games and Culture. Their Turkish names are Ekonomi, Teknoloji, Bilim, Oyun and Kültür, and their shared keys are economy, technology, science, games and culture.

Politics, foreign policy, sports and similar high risk topics are not covered. The site consists of safe and interesting stories.

## Publishing rhythm

The target is three or four stories per week. There are no issues, numbered editions or fixed daily or weekly slots. Each run publishes one piece, a news story or an explainer, as set in docs/agent-manual.md. Quality takes priority over the target, so a weak story is not published to fill a gap.

## Sources

Every factual claim is traceable to a named source, including background facts, and the sources are shown with the article. Primary sources are preferred, such as official announcements, company filings and research papers. Claims from a single weak source are not reported as fact. Text is written from the facts and is not copied from the sources.

A source counts only if its page was opened and read. Anything attributed to a person or a publication must appear on the cited page. Every story is checked against its sources by someone other than its writer before it is published.

## Economy stories

Economy stories report what happened. They never tell readers to buy, sell or hold anything, and they do not predict prices as if the outcome were known. Bitpulse does not give investment advice.

## Corrections

Mistakes are fixed openly. A corrected article shows a dated correction note that says what was wrong. Silent changes to facts are not allowed.

## Images

Every story has an image. The first choice is an image directly tied to the story, such as a photo released by a space agency or a photo of the product. The second choice is a stock or general image about the topic. A general image must be honest about what it shows, so its description names what is really in the frame and it never suggests a person or a thing was part of the event when it was not.

An image is used only when its license clearly allows the use, and its source and license are shown next to it. Wikimedia Commons, public agencies and stock libraries with clear terms are good sources. An image with unclear rights is never used, which rules out film stills, game screenshots, press kit images and news agency photos unless their terms clearly allow reuse.

An image fills its whole frame with a real scene. A cut out object on a white, plain or transparent background is not used, because it breaks the look of the pages. Product photos work only when the product sits in a real setting.

Images are JPEG or PNG in landscape format. The original must be sharp and larger than 2000 pixels wide, and the stored copy is exactly 2000 pixels wide, so it stays clear on large screens without slowing the page.

Social posts follow a separate image rule. An image on a social post shows the event, the people, the place or the product of the story itself, such as a photo from the event, an image released by the organization in the story, a film still or a game screenshot. A general or stock image is not used there. Each image is credited to its owner in the post, and when it was taken from a news report, the outlet is named as well. Social images are linked by their address and never stored in this repository or shown on the site.

## Disclosure and bylines

The site states clearly that its articles are written by artificial intelligence and that it is operated by Claude and its subagents, with a project owner who starts the runs and approves changes to the design, the site code and the rules. Articles have no byline, and the site has no author pages, author boxes, team sections or personal names.

## Languages

Each story is published in English and Turkish with the same facts and the same sources. Each language version is written to read naturally and is not a word for word translation.
