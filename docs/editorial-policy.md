# Editorial policy

These rules apply to everything published on Bitpulse.

## Scope

Bitpulse is a world news site. Anything that happens in the world can be a Bitpulse story, whatever its subject, as long as it can be reported under these rules. A story is welcome whenever it happened, including today. Explainers answer lasting questions under the same rules. How stories sound is defined in docs/style-guide.md.

## Categories

There are seven categories: World, Economy, Technology, Science, Health, Culture and Sports. Their Turkish names are Dünya, Ekonomi, Teknoloji, Bilim, Sağlık, Kültür and Spor, and their shared keys are world, economy, technology, science, health, culture and sports. Every story belongs to exactly one category, the one its main subject fits best.

World covers politics, elections, governments, diplomacy, conflicts, law and courts, crime, disasters and society. Economy covers companies, markets, prices, jobs, trade and the wider economy. Technology covers artificial intelligence, robots, devices, chips, vehicles, aviation and the internet. Science covers space, nature and wildlife, physics, climate and the Earth. A natural event is a Science story when the story is about the event itself, and a World story when it is about its effect on people. Health covers medicine, diseases, public health and medical research. Culture covers film, television, music, books, video games, internet culture and the arts. Sports covers competitions, results, athletes and the business of sport.

## Neutrality

Bitpulse takes no side. It reports what happened, who said what and what is known, and it leaves the judgment to the reader.

- A fact and a claim are kept apart. A claim is attributed to the person or the body that made it, and a disputed claim is called disputed.
- When sides disagree, whether they are countries, parties, companies or people, each side's position is given, in its own words where possible and with comparable space. When a side did not respond or could not be reached, the story says so.
- Words that carry a judgment, such as regime, terrorist, massacre or freedom fighter, appear only inside a quote attributed to its speaker. A designation made by a government or an international body is reported as that body's designation. Reporting verbs stay neutral: a person says, not admits, claims or slams.
- Casualty, damage and crowd figures are attributed to their source. When sources differ, each figure is given with its source or the range is shown. A figure from a party to a conflict is labeled as such.
- A contested story is never built only on sources from one side. Its sources come from more than one side and, for an international story, from more than one region.
- Election and political stories report results, positions, decisions and attributed polls. They never endorse anyone and never state a prediction as fact.
- Bitpulse publishes no opinion pieces. In a contested story, interpretation is limited to context the sources support, and the story never decides who is right.
- A disputed place is named as major international outlets name it, and its other name is given when the dispute is part of the story.
- Headlines, summaries, images and social posts follow the same rules. An image is chosen to show the event, never to make one side look better or worse.

## Sensitive stories

A private person who appears in a sensitive story is named only by their initials, so Jane Doe becomes J.D. Sensitive stories are those about crime, court cases, accidents, disasters, illness, death and any other situation where being named could harm the person. The rule covers suspects, accused people, victims, witnesses and relatives, in both languages and everywhere the person appears: the headline, the summary, the text, image descriptions and social posts.

The rule does not cover public figures acting in their public role, such as politicians, officials, executives, celebrities and athletes.

A child in a sensitive story is not named, not even by initials. At most their age is given.

Nothing else that identifies a person named by initials is published, such as their face, home address, workplace or the names of family members.

A person accused of a crime is described as a suspect or as accused until a court decides. A charge is never reported as a fact.

## Graphic content

Images and videos never show blood, dead or injured bodies, executions or the moment people are harmed. A violent event is shown through its context instead: the place afterwards, emergency services, officials, maps or the people involved before or after the event. The text describes violence plainly and without gruesome detail. The same rules apply to social posts.

## Publishing rhythm

The target is five stories per day. There are no issues, numbered editions or fixed slots. Quality takes priority over the target, so a weak story is not published to fill a gap.

## Sources

Every factual claim is traceable to a named source, including background facts, and the sources are shown with the article. Primary sources are preferred, such as official announcements, company filings, court documents and research papers. Claims from a single weak source are not reported as fact. Text is written from the facts and is not copied from the sources.

A source counts only if its page was opened and read. Anything attributed to a person or a publication must appear on the cited page. When a fact was first reported by a news outlet, that outlet is named as a source, and Bitpulse never presents another outlet's reporting as its own. Every claim is checked against its source page before the story is published, and the editor reads every story before it goes live.

A story about an event that is still unfolding says clearly what is confirmed and what is not.

## Updates, new developments and corrections

A story that grows is updated at the same address. A short note at the top gives the time of the update and what changed. When the editor judges an update important, the story moves up on the home page and carries an Updated label.

A new development that is a story of its own, such as a deal that collapses days after it was signed, gets a new story at a new address. The new story links to the earlier one, and the earlier one gets a link to the new development.

Mistakes are fixed openly. A corrected article shows a dated correction note that says what was wrong. Silent changes to facts are not allowed. A correction is not an update: it does not move the story on the home page, and corrected stories are listed on the corrections page.

## Economy stories

Economy stories report what happened. They never tell readers to buy, sell or hold anything, and they do not predict prices as if the outcome were known. Bitpulse does not give investment advice.

## Images

Every story has an image, and the first choice is an image directly tied to the story. Any image may be used, including press images, film stills, game screenshots, trailer frames and news agency photos, as long as its owner and its source are shown next to it. When an image has no open license, the license field gives its terms as the source states them and links to the source page. Whether an image is free to use is never a reason to choose or drop a story. The rules on graphic content and on sensitive stories come first.

A general image about the topic must be honest about what it shows, so its description names what is really in the frame and it never suggests a person or a thing was part of the event when it was not.

An image fills its whole frame with a real scene. A cut out object on a white, plain or transparent background is not used, because it breaks the look of the pages. Product photos work only when the product sits in a real setting.

Images are JPEG or PNG in landscape format. The original must be sharp and at least 1200 pixels wide. The stored copy keeps the original width up to 2000 pixels and is never enlarged, so it stays clear without slowing the page. An image of the story's own subject at 1200 pixels is better than a larger image of something else.

Social posts use images the same way. An image on a social post shows the event, the people, the place or the product of the story itself, and a general or stock image is not used there. Each image is credited to its owner in the post, and when it was taken from a news report, the outlet is named as well. Social images are linked by their address and never stored in this repository or shown on the site. A social video is footage of the event of the story, or for an economy story, footage of what the story is about. A slow event may be sped up, and the caption says so. It is credited to its owner in the caption and linked by its address.

## Disclosure and bylines

This repository states openly that the stories are chosen by the project owner and researched with the help of AI. The live website and the social posts do not state this. Articles have no byline, and the site has no author pages, author boxes, team sections or personal names.

## Languages

Each story is published in English and Turkish with the same facts and the same sources. Each language version is written to read naturally and is not a word for word translation.
