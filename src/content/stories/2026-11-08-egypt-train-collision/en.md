Seven people were killed and more than 100 were injured when two trains collided on Sunday in al-Ayyat district, Giza province, about 30 miles south of Cairo, officials said.

According to Egypt's transport ministry, a train travelling to the south-western Fayoum province crashed into the rear of a train heading to the southern province of Aswan. An inspection indicated that the Cairo-Aswan train had been stationary on the same track, officials said.

## What is known and what is not

The Guardian's report gives no cause for the collision. It does not say why the Cairo-Aswan train was standing on the track or how the second train came to hit it. It also does not say how many people were aboard either train or how serious the injuries are. This story draws only on that one report, so the toll and the account of the crash may change as officials say more.

A.G., a resident of Kafr Girza in al-Ayyat, said people rushed to the scene, pulled out survivors and called ambulances. The resident said they saw about 80 of the injured taken to hospital by ambulance, and that some passengers were trapped inside one of the carriages of the stationary train and died.

Egypt's president, Abdel Fatah al-Sisi, said in a social media post that he had ordered a prompt investigation. The Egyptian railway authority said emergency teams and cranes had been sent to clear the wreckage and restore train services to their normal schedule as soon as possible.

## Background

The Guardian notes that train derailments and crashes are common in Egypt, where the railway system has a history of mismanagement and poor maintenance of equipment. In recent years the government has announced modernisation initiatives to improve its railways. Last September, a train driver and his assistant were injured after two freight trains collided in the same district.

Egypt's deadliest train crash was in 2002, when more than 300 people were killed after a fire broke out on an overnight train journeying from Cairo to southern Egypt, the Guardian reports.
