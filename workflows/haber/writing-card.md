# Writing card

Everything a writer needs to turn a radar brief into a Bitpulse news story. The full rules are in docs/editorial-policy.md and docs/style-guide.md. Open them only when this card does not answer a question.

## Facts

- Every fact comes from the brief. Nothing is added from memory. A fact the brief does not hold is left out.
- A claim belongs to whoever made it: "the company said", "according to police". A disputed claim is called disputed.
- When sides disagree, each side's position is given with comparable space. A side that did not respond is said to have not responded.
- Casualty and damage figures are attributed. Different figures from different sources are each given with their source.
- Judgment words such as regime, terrorist, massacre or freedom fighter appear only inside a quote. Reporting verbs stay neutral: says, not admits, claims or slams.
- A suspect is a suspect until a court decides.
- A private person in a story about crime, a court case, an accident, a disaster, illness or death is named only by initials, such as J.D. Children are not named at all, at most their age is given. Public figures in their public role keep their full names.
- No gruesome detail. Violence is described plainly.
- An unfolding event says clearly what is confirmed and what is not.
- Economy stories give no investment advice and no price predictions.
- The text never mentions how it was researched or written, and never mentions AI.

## Shape

- 300 to 500 words in English, the Turkish version usually a little shorter. Never add details only to reach a length. If a sentence gives the reader nothing new, it goes.
- The first paragraph says what happened. Then why it matters, what a reader needs to follow it, and what happens next if known.
- One or two short subheadings at most, written as `## ` lines, plain and specific.
- No first person, no hype, no talking down. Warm, calm, confident, like a well informed friend.
- Technical words only when needed, explained in the same or the next sentence.
- A number stays only if a general reader can feel what it means.

## Headline and summary

- The headline names the people or organizations involved and points to the change or tension. It never exaggerates and never hides the subject to force a click.
- English: 50 to 65 characters, never more than 70. Turkish: 55 to 70, never more than 75.
- English headlines capitalize every word except short articles, conjunctions and prepositions: Astronauts Unroll the Space Station's Last New Solar Array.
- Turkish headlines are written as a sentence, only the first word and proper names capitalized: Nintendo yazılım satışlarının yüzde 60,7'sini dijitalden yaptı.
- One clause, at most one comma or colon, no "and" joining two points. The most important name comes first.
- The summary under it adds a reason to keep reading and does not repeat the headline. One or two sentences.

## Turkish version

- The same facts and sources, written to read naturally in Turkish. Never a word for word translation.
- Headline and summary follow the same rules with the Turkish lengths.
- Numbers use Turkish formatting: 1,8 milyon, yüzde 60,7.

## Frontmatter fields

- slug: lowercase English words with dashes for en.md, lowercase Turkish words with dashes and no Turkish letters for tr.md. Both unique and short.
- title and summary: in double quotes.
- category: one of world, economy, technology, science, health, culture, sports. The same in both files.
- image alt: what is really in the frame, one or two sentences. credit: the owner as the source names it. license: the terms as the source states them, or "All rights reserved" with licenseUrl pointing to the source page.
- date, time, kind, sources and image file are already filled in. Do not change them.
