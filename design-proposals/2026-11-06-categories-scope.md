# Proposal: keep the five categories and sharpen the scope around footage

Status: open, waiting for review.

Branch: proposal/categories-scope

This changes the scope text in docs/editorial-policy.md and two paragraphs in src/pages/Policy.tsx. It keeps the five categories, their names and their keys, so the menu does not change and no screenshots are needed. An alternative that would change the menu is described under Risks, and it would need screenshots before approval.

## The request

Research which news categories draw the most interest worldwide, on social media and in search, for a site like Bitpulse. Take into account that news stories are now chosen only when real footage of the event exists. Use the research to propose the categories and the scope of Bitpulse, with the evidence for it.

## The evidence

Site statistics are about 140 views a day including the owner and about 3 search clicks a week. They are too small to show demand, so none are used. All figures below come from pages that were opened and read on 2026-11-06. Claims that rest on one source, on a company's own number or on a secondary page are marked.

### 1. News reaches readers as video on social platforms, and Instagram and YouTube matter most for this format

- Reuters Institute, [Digital News Report 2026, overview](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary), 16 June 2026: across 48 markets, social media and video networks (54%) now lead news websites and apps (51%) as a way to reach news. 77% watch online news video weekly. For news, 34% use YouTube, 26% use Instagram (42% of 18 to 24 year olds, the biggest social platform for news in that age group) and 20% use TikTok. The page gives no figure for X across markets, and X news use in the US fell by 7 points. The page text was cut off near its end, so topic data in the last 5,000 characters was not read.
- Reuters Institute, [Digital News Report 2025, executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary): weekly social video news use rose from 52% in 2020 to 65% in 2025. 40% of people sometimes or often avoid the news, and 61% do in Turkey.
- Pew Research Center, [1 in 5 Americans now regularly get news on TikTok](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/09/25/1-in-5-americans-now-regularly-get-news-on-tiktok-up-sharply-from-2020/): 20% of US adults regularly get news on TikTok, up from 3% in 2020. The opened page showed an October 2026 date and a 2026 survey, while its address says September 2025, so the year of the update is uncertain. The 20% and 3% figures are the same in both readings. US only.

What this means: a vertical clip is the right format for the audience that is growing. It says nothing about which topic wins.

### 2. Which topics do people want, according to surveys

Direct, recent, worldwide data on interest by topic was not found. The Reuters Institute topic charts for 2024 and 2025 could not be opened, and the summaries of both years give no topic figures. What was found is indirect:

- Reuters Institute, [Digital News Report 2023 (full report, PDF, opened through a mirror)](https://static.poder360.com.br/2023/06/Digital-News-Report-Reuters-2023.pdf): among US news avoiders, science and technology news was the topic avoided least, by 4% of avoiders on the left and 8% on the right. Sport (39% and 44%), entertainment and celebrity news (39% and 39%) and national politics (33% and 35%) were avoided far more. The same report says that selective avoiders are most interested in positive news (55% of avoiders), solutions (46%) and explainers (39%). US only, 2023, avoidance and not interest.
- Pew Research Center, [A closer look at the topics of news influencers' posts](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/11/18/a-closer-look-at-the-topics-of-news-influencers-posts/), 18 November 2024: of influencer posts about current events, 55% were about government, politics or the election, 18% social issues, 14% international issues and 3% technology. This shows what the crowded part of social news is. These are topics Bitpulse excludes, and technology is almost absent from that feed. US only, influencers only, and it measures what is posted, not what is wanted.
- Reuters Institute, [Digital News Report 2024 as summarised by DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-global-news-report), 31 July 2024: interest in politics fell from 45% in 2018 to 30% in 2024 across 12 core markets. Secondary source, politics only.

Weak point: nothing opened gives a worldwide ranking of Economy, Technology, Science, Games and Culture by interest. Any such ranking in this proposal is built from the search and video data below.

### 3. Search

- Google, [Year in Search 2025](https://trends.withgoogle.com/year-in-search/2025/), global list as shown on the opened page (the page labelled its region selector "Global" next to "Argentina", so the region of the list is slightly uncertain). Google ranks by growth in searches, not by total volume. Top searches: Gemini first, DeepSeek sixth, iPhone 17 ninth. The rest of the top ten were cricket and football tournaments, Charlie Kirk, Iran and India and Pakistan. Games: Arc Raiders, Battlefield 6, Strands, Split Fiction and Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 led, with Grand Theft Auto 6 seventh. Movies: Anora, Superman and A Minecraft Movie led. TV: Monster: The Ed Gein Story, Squid Game 3 and Adolescence led. No science or consumer technology list appeared on the opened page. The News list was led by politics, Iran, a new pope, fires and a tsunami, and none of its ten items was an economy story.
- Google, [Year in Search 2025 blog post](https://blog.google/products/search/year-in-search-2025/): searches starting "How do I" reached an all time high, up 25%, "Tell me about" was up 70% and "What's the deal with" rose more than ever. This favours explainers.

What this means: in search, the strongest non-sport, non-political growth is in AI and phones (Technology), games and film and television (Games, Culture). Science did not appear as a category list on the page that was read. No economy item appeared in the opened News list or the top ten.

### 4. Social video and attention, by category

- Games. Rockstar Games said its second Grand Theft Auto VI trailer passed 475 million views across all platforms in 24 hours ([Wccftech, 8 May 2025](https://wccftech.com/gta-vi-trailer-2-breaks-all-time-24h-viewership-records-beating-deadpool-vs-wolverine/), company figure, not verified independently). On YouTube alone the first trailer had 90,421,491 views in 24 hours and the second 75,913,751 ([Epicflix](https://epicflix.com/grand-theft-auto-vi-trailer-2-falls-short-of-breaking-youtube-record-set-by-first-trailer), single secondary source). Streams Charts reported 6.3 million peak concurrent viewers for the Nintendo Switch 2 showcase on 2 April 2025, ahead of the Apple and Samsung events and behind only the 2020 PlayStation 5 reveal ([Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/switch-2-showcase)).
- Consumer technology. The iPhone 17 event peaked at over 2.8 million viewers on 9 September 2025, the fourth largest Apple event, and Samsung's January 2025 Unpacked at about 582,000 ([Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/apple-event-2025-iphone-17-revealed)). These are presentations, and the footage rule excludes a presentation.
- Space and science. NASA said the Artemis II launch on 1 April 2026 had 23.9 million views and 16.6 million live viewers, a peak of about 3.66 million concurrent viewers, and the launch, flyby and splashdown together passed 149.4 million views on NASA platforms ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/general/nasas-artemis-ii-breaks-agency-streaming-record/)). The all time YouTube live record is listed as 8 million concurrent viewers for India's Chandrayaan-3 landing ([Dexerto, updated 30 October 2026](https://dexerto.com/entertainment/livestream-viewership-records-all-time-peak-viewers-twitch-youtube-1493241), list sourced to PlayBoard, undated entry). TikTok said STEM hashtags had passed 110 billion views ([TechCrunch, 14 March 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/14/tiktok-is-adding-a-dedicated-feed-for-stem-content), company figure, old).
- Robots. A clip of a Unitree G1 humanoid robot passed 80 million views on X in July 2025 ([Interesting Engineering, 18 July 2025](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/humanoid-robot-china-uncle-bot-viral-sensation)). Single source, one clip.
- Overall YouTube mix. Music had about 22% of YouTube views and gaming about 8% in the Pex analysis ([Hypebot, 11 August 2020](https://www.hypebot.com/new-data-shows-music-videos-continue-to-dominate-youtube/)). The data is from about 2019 and no newer category split could be opened, so it is only a rough guide that gaming is a large video category.

### 5. What was not found

No opened source gave a category ranking for X or Instagram Reels, a Similarweb or Chartbeat split by topic, or recent search volume for science and economy. Searches for them returned only summaries, which were not relied on. The finding rests on the pieces above, and it points in one direction without being a measured ranking.

### Reading of the evidence

1. The format is settled by reach. News is moving to video on Instagram and YouTube, and a clip fits it.
2. The crowded, high interest topics on social news are politics and conflict, which Bitpulse excludes. Technology is only 3% of influencer news posts, so there is room.
3. Where attention is largest and growing and the topic is allowed, it is in AI and phones, games and trailers, film and television, space and robots. Each of these comes with film of the event.
4. Science and technology are the least avoided news topics in the one dataset found, and explainers are the form avoiders say they want most, after positive and solution stories. Search phrasing is shifting to "how" and "what's the deal" questions.
5. Economy has the weakest support. It had no item in the opened Google lists, and a business story usually has no film of the event. No opened source shows demand for economy news from a general audience.

## What footage-first means for each category

The figure for each category is a judgment from the evidence above and from what a clip needs to show. It is not a measured share.

| Category | Footage that exists on its own | Fit with a vertical clip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology | Robot demonstrations, vehicle tests, drone and aircraft flights, device teardowns, AI products working on screen. Launch keynotes are presentations and do not count. | Strong, if the clip shows the thing doing something. |
| Science | Rocket launches and landings, telescopes and probes, wildlife and nature in the field, lab experiments, eclipses and aurora. | Strong. Agencies release the footage, which also helps the rights check. |
| Games | Trailers, gameplay reveals and event footage. | Strong. A trailer is one unbroken clip from the owner. The rights for a trailer have to match the existing social rule. |
| Culture | Film and series trailers, premieres, concerts, museum and exhibition openings. | Strong for trailers and events, weak for books and essays. |
| Economy | A factory or a port in operation, a product going on sale, a store queue, a chip plant opening. | Weak. Most economy news is a number, a filing or a statement. Stories qualify only when the event itself was filmed. |

A footage test favours stories that are visible. That is a bias toward spectacle. The scope below limits it by tying each category to a focus and by sending figure driven subjects to explainers, which need no clip and are not shared on social media.

## The proposal

Keep the five categories, with the same English and Turkish names and keys:

| Key | English | Turkish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| economy | Economy | Ekonomi |
| technology | Technology | Teknoloji |
| science | Science | Bilim |
| games | Games | Oyun |
| culture | Culture | Kültür |

Change the scope in three ways. Each category gets a one sentence focus that follows the evidence: AI, robots, devices, chips, vehicles and aviation for Technology, space, nature, health research and the Earth for Science, trailers, hardware and studios for Games, film, television, music and internet culture for Culture. Economy is narrowed to companies, products, prices and trade where they connect to the other four. The footage rule is written into the scope, and subjects with no footage move to explainers. The exclusions stay as they are, and the sentence about natural events moves into the Science focus.

### New Scope text for docs/editorial-policy.md

The branch proposal/categories-scope holds the full file with only this section changed. The section reads:

> ## Scope
>
> Bitpulse is a news site that explains technology, science, games and culture to curious general readers, and it covers the economy where it shapes them. A story is welcome whenever it happened, including today, as long as its subject is one of these. Explainers answer lasting questions under the same rules. How stories sound is defined in docs/style-guide.md.
>
> There are five categories: Economy, Technology, Science, Games and Culture. Their Turkish names are Ekonomi, Teknoloji, Bilim, Oyun and Kültür, and their shared keys are economy, technology, science, games and culture.
>
> Each category has a focus. Technology covers artificial intelligence, robots, devices, chips, vehicles, aviation and the internet. Science covers space, nature and wildlife, health and medical research, physics and the Earth, and a natural event can be a science story when the story is about the event itself and not the harm it caused. Games covers new games, trailers, consoles and hardware, studios and players. Culture covers film, television, music, books, internet culture and the arts. Economy covers companies, products, prices and trade where they connect to technology, science, games or culture. Everyday money questions are answered in explainers.
>
> A news story is told only when its event was filmed, as set in docs/agent-manual.md, so news stories come mostly from what can be seen happening: launches, demonstrations, trailers, gameplay, premieres, new machines and animals or places in the field. A change in figures, a filing or a regulation has no such footage, so it is covered in an explainer, or not at all.
>
> Party politics, elections, government disputes, foreign policy, war and sports results are not covered. Stories whose subject is harm to people, such as crime, violence, accidents and disasters, are not covered either. A story is not excluded only because a government, a law or a controversy plays a part in it.

- src/pages/Policy.tsx, the "What Bitpulse covers" section, English and Turkish. Locked as well.

- No change to src/stories.ts, src/i18n.ts, the menu, the category pages or the colors. The keys, names and order stay the same. The category descriptions in src/i18n.ts already read "Markets and companies shaping technology" for Economy, which matches the narrowed Economy focus.

The site looks the same. The only visible change is longer text on the policy page, which has no layout change, so no screenshots are needed. If the review wants to change the categories themselves (see Risks), the files would be src/stories.ts (the categories list), src/i18n.ts (names and descriptions), the menu and category pages, any story whose category changes, and the sitemap and tests that list categories. Screenshots of the menu and the category pages, current and proposed, would be needed before approval.

## Risks

- The ranking of categories is a judgment. No opened source ranks Economy, Technology, Science, Games and Culture by worldwide interest, so the claim that Economy is weakest rests on footage and on its absence from search and social lists, not on a survey. Reviewers should treat it as an inference.
- Footage first favours spectacle. It can pull the site toward robots falling over, rocket launches and trailers. The focus lines and the explainer route are the counterweight, and the editor role should check that a story has something to explain beyond the clip.
- Economy may end up with very few news stories. That is intended for now. After about 30 published stories, the category counts can be reviewed. If Economy has fewer than about three news stories, merging it into the other four, or replacing it with a category such as Space or Mobility, could be proposed with the screenshots that a menu change needs. That option is not proposed here because the evidence for a specific replacement is too thin: space data is strong but is already inside Science, and no opened source supports a Mobility category.
- Games and Culture news depends on trailers. Trailer rights, which the social rules handle, and the dependence on a few publishers' release calendars are a risk to steady output.
- Presentations are excluded by the footage rule, so the biggest consumer technology events (keynotes, with 0.58 to 2.8 million live viewers in the Streams Charts figures) cannot be stories on their own. A product's later demonstration can.
- The sources are mostly company figures and secondary summaries, and several are US only. The Reuters 2025 and 2026 reports were read only through their summary pages. The worldwide audience Bitpulse targets, including Turkey, is covered by one avoidance figure and Google's global list.
- A narrower focus excludes some stories that earlier runs would have accepted, such as pure market stories. Existing economy stories stay and are not affected.
